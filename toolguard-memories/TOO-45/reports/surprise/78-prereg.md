## Contamination — the sharpest disclosure of the series so far, and it names causation

Asked to declare unbidden context *and whether it informed the estimate*, this estimator did both, per item:

- **Project `CLAUDE.md`** — *"told me the test framework is `unittest`, that runtime is stdlib-only, that all bash parsing goes through the PEG grammar (**which is why I excluded the parser package from the touch set**), and that documentation review is a push-time gate (**which is why `README.md` is only medium**)."*
- **Global `CLAUDE.md`** — informed the uncertainties file's direction-of-error note.
- **Auto-memory index** — *"shaped tone more than content. No entry named ticket 78 or its files."*
- **A git-status snapshot** of untracked filenames — truncated, named nothing relevant here.

**Two specific predictions were caused by injected context**, not by the ticket or the inventory. That is no longer an abstract caveat: it is a measured effect on the artefact being scored.

**Method note worth keeping:** the earlier form of the ask — "declare what you read" — produced a vague acknowledgement. Asking for **"and whether it informed your estimate"** produced this. The wording of the disclosure request is itself an instrument, and the better version should be used for the rest of the series.

Note also that auto-memory has now been checked against the item and found irrelevant twice. The *growing contamination* concern recorded on ticket 77 remains real in principle, but two data points suggest auto-memory is a weaker channel than the project `CLAUDE.md`, which is **static across the series** and therefore does not confound the trend the way a growing file would.
