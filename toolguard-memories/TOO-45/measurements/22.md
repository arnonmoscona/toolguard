## SEQUENCING NOTE, 2026-08-21 — §5 is the same defect as ticket 20a, and 20a should supply the vocabulary

§5 above ends by noting that a never-exercised rule is indistinguishable from a genuinely covered one, *"same anti-vacuity shape as ticket 20's no-corpus branch returning `True`."* That is not merely an analogy — **it is the same defect, and it now has a decided fix.**

Ticket 20a introduces a three-state safety result (`safe` / `unsafe` / `unverified`) precisely so that *"the corpus checked this and it was clean"* stops collapsing into *"the corpus never looked."* §5 needs exactly that distinction: a corpus-redundancy finding must be able to say **"covered"** versus **"never exercised"**, and today it says the first while meaning either.

**Consequences:**

- **Keep 20a ahead of 22 in the queue.** It is already ordered that way; this is the reason, and it should not be reshuffled for convenience.
- **22 must reuse 20a's states rather than inventing a parallel set.** Two vocabularies for one concept is *"one concept, many enumerations"* — the failure this campaign has now hit repeatedly, most expensively in ticket 79's four hand-written verdict enumerations.
- Whoever implements 22 should be told 20a's constants exist and be required to justify any new state rather than adding one by default.

This also sharpens the *"the report must stop reading as a delete-list"* finding above: a finding marked `unverified` is one an operator must **not** act on by deleting, and that distinction is invisible in today's output.
